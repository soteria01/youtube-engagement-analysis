# youtube-engagement-analysis
YouTube engagement analysis comparing movie teasers, trailers, and influencer reviews using Velma-related videos. Includes dataset, Power BI visuals, insights, and recommendations.
YouTube Engagement Analysis: Teasers vs Trailers (Velma Case Study)
By Soteria Ebulu
**Project Overview
**Movie studios rely heavily on online engagement to build anticipation and drive viewership.
This project analyzes YouTube engagement metrics for four Velma‑related videos — including the official teaser, official trailer, and influencer reviews — to understand how different video formats influence audience behavior.

The goal was to compare views, likes, and comments across teasers, trailers, and review content, and uncover insights filmmakers can use to improve promotional strategies.

🔍 Insights & Interpretation
1. The official trailer dominated viewership
The Velma Official Trailer received 31M+ views, far surpassing the teaser and review videos — even with comments disabled.

“The official trailer had the most engagements even with a disabled comment section.”

2. Teasers spark conversation
The Velma Official Teaser generated 96K comments, the highest comment count in the dataset.
Teasers tend to create curiosity and discussion.

3. Influencer reviews drive interaction
Although review videos had fewer views, they produced strong comment engagement — especially negative reviews.
Opinion‑based content still drives meaningful interaction.

“The video with least views… still had engagements when it came to comments.”

4. All engagement is valuable
Even negative reviews contributed to visibility and conversation around the film.

🎯 Conclusion & Recommendations
Filmmakers can improve engagement by:

Combining official promotional content with influencer reviews

Using teasers to build anticipation

Using trailers to deliver detail and drive high view counts

Incorporating hashtags and cross‑platform promotion

Leveraging social media influencers to amplify reach

“Films can be promoted through YouTube reviews, written articles, live streaming, [and] social media influencers.”
